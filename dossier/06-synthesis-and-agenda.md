# Synthesis and the evidence that would change it

## A market thesis with a technical dependency

The commercial proposition becomes clearer when tied to a buyer's task and a verifiable output. A migration programme may need discovery, integration, identity changes and assurance; an algorithm implementation is not a substitute for every one of those outputs. The converse is equally important: capabilities distributed through existing products may reduce the standalone market available to a new supplier.

The review therefore keeps **sector demand**, **incremental spending**, **vendor differentiation** and **company-specific value capture** as separate propositions. A forecast can motivate further work but cannot close the chain among them. [TH01–TH02](theses.json)

## The central technical dependency

For a supplier claim to become persuasive, evidence must connect construction assumptions to implementation behavior, protocol integration and the intended operating environment. Formal results, controlled benchmarks and provider deployment accounts each help with part of that chain. They do not collapse into a single assurance label.

The most useful comparisons preserve function and conditions. A protocol redesign is evaluated against its own construction; an embedded implementation against its resource envelope; a transport configuration against its network and authentication assumptions. [TH03–TH04](theses.json)

## An argument against overconfidence

There are two symmetrical mistakes. One infers a large, captured market from urgent security guidance. The other dismisses preparation because the arrival date of a capable quantum computer is uncertain. The reviewed material supports a more bounded position: plan with explicit confidentiality and migration assumptions, investigate actual budget ownership, and demand evidence for the configuration and outcome under discussion. [TH05](theses.json)

## Decisive next records

| Research question | Next record | What would change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How large is the comparable market? | Reconciled definitions, revenue boundaries and transparent methods | Permit a justified comparison rather than headline averaging |
| Where is new spending occurring? | Buyer-level procurement and renewal data without duplicate bundles | Separate incremental demand from replacement work |
| What distinguishes a supplier? | Named configuration, deployment boundary, outcome and maintained support | Support a company-specific commercial argument |
| Which implementation claim is justified? | Reproducible fixture with positive/negative controls and assurance scope | Replace attributed or literature-level statements with bounded measurements |
| Which systems are hardest to change? | Dependency, updateability and lifecycle records for the intended environment | Turn a general migration theme into a prioritized programme |

## Relationship to the supplied CES2025 work

The original presentation supplies the starting questions and company examples. The later corpus broadens them into a sector review. Dates and access scopes remain attached to the sources, so later publications are not represented as material available at the original event.

The existing synthetic model is a small demonstrator of planning sensitivity. Its result is not a product benchmark or a measure of the market. The paper atlas and analyst desk make the broader evidence inspectable; the [thesis register](theses.json) keeps interpretation and countercases explicit.
